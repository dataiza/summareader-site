What these terms cover

These terms cover the information published on these pages about SummaReader: a product page, a user guide, the privacy policy and the end-user licence. The app itself is covered by its end-user licence, not by this page. If the two differ, the end-user licence applies.

The app and its licence

SummaReader is proprietary; see the end-user licence. The licence says what you may and may not do with the app, in its free and its Premium edition.

The sync server is a separate program under AGPL-3.0. Open-source components inside the app stay under their own licences; their notices are in the app, at Configuration › About › Open-source licences.

Where the app is distributed

We distribute the app only through:

  • GitHub releases of the repository dataiza/summareader-release (free edition, Linux and Android)
  • Google Play
  • Apple TestFlight (test versions)
  • the Apple App Store

Not every edition is on every channel at every time. A copy from another source is not from us.

There is no service

We run nothing for you. There is no account, no hosted sync, no summaries on our servers and no search on our servers. Your library is on your devices. If you use sync, the server is one that you or a person you trust operates.

So there is no service for us to stop or change, and nothing of yours for us to lose. There is also nobody who can restore your data for you; see the recovery code in the guide.

What you are responsible for

  • The sources you add. The app gets what you point it at, directly. You follow each site's terms, including how often you may get its content. YouTube access in the app is unofficial; see the end-user licence.
  • Any endpoint you set. If you send items to a provider for summaries, the item is sent there under that provider's terms, with your own key. We are not a party to it.
  • Your keys. The encryption key and the recovery code are only on your devices. If you lose them, nobody can recover your synced data.
  • What you do with what you read. Summaries are written by a language model. They can be wrong.
